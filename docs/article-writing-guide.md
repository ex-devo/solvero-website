# Solvero article writing guide

Status: article voice agreed. Updated 2026-10-27.

Apply this guidance when drafting, updating or refining articles. Website marketing guidance lives in `website-copy-guide.md`.

## Editorial conventions

Use UK English spelling, including colour, prioritise and revolutionise.

## Agreed direction

Direct, conversational and thoughtful. Help readers understand the subject and its practical implications. Give clear recommendations with reasoning. Explore new developments with curiosity and measured judgement. Distinguish fact from interpretation.

Write for readers who understand their business. Explain unfamiliar process or technology concepts in everyday language, introducing technical terms when they help. Make clear recommendations when the evidence supports them, explaining the reasoning and any uncertainty. Let expertise come through in useful advice.

Use a direct, conversational voice that helps the reader think through the subject, with room for reasoning, examples and technical detail according to their needs. Open practical articles with a concrete observation, then develop the reasoning. An occasional memorable observation can give the writing character; explain its point concretely.

For technology news and commentary, use a measured, curious voice. Explain what is known, explore the implications and include our assessment of what the development means for businesses. Clearly distinguish our interpretation from established facts.

Articles should provide useful advice in their own right. Connect the subject to Solvero's services in a restrained, relevant way where that connection follows naturally.

Ground business descriptions and experience claims in the positioning and evidence requirements in `README.md`. Use “we” when describing Solvero's services, approach and work. Distinguish illustrative examples from verified results.

## Authentic Writing

Use the repository copy of [Authentic Writing](editorial/authentic-writing/SKILL.md) when drafting, rewriting or reviewing articles. Read its [rhythm guidance](editorial/authentic-writing/references/rhythm-system.md) alongside it. These files preserve the guidance supplied in `Authentic writing-v1`; keeping them here makes the same instructions available on every machine. Apply the guidance to introductions, headings, captions, metadata and calls to action as well as the main text.

Use simple, specific language and natural contractions. Vary sentence length and paragraph length to suit the thought. Paragraphs usually have 1–2 sentences, with 3 as the ceiling; develop longer explanations across paragraphs. Read the finished piece for rhythm, repeated sentence openings and weak endings. Use digits for numbers, UK spelling and punctuation without em dashes.

Read the complete article before editing. Cut filler, mechanical transitions, inflated claims, staged contrasts and repeated conclusions. Keep the measured, thoughtful Solvero voice: concrete verbs and occasional asides should clarify the subject and sound natural. Run the skill's language and rhythm self-check before delivering the draft.

Give the subject enough space. Preserve supported claims, qualifications, reasoning, examples and the writer's judgement. Tighten wording while keeping source links, technical instructions, code, calculations and worksheet data intact. Check the rewrite against its sources for lost facts or unsupported additions. Article length follows the explanation readers need; there is no target percentage reduction.

Use the Markdown headings, lists, tables, linked sources, images and captions required by this guide and the site renderer. Keep technical names, source titles, code and data accurate. Apply Authentic Writing's voice and rhythm within that article format, with this guide and README.md governing structure, depth, positioning and evidence. Deliver only the finished article text in the manuscript.

## Reading and navigation

Use descriptive headings in sentence case and paragraphs that each develop one point. Use lists for steps or comparable items, and tables when readers need to compare values or choices. Reserve bold for information that needs emphasis. A list does not need a bold label on every item.

Article pages have a Solvero Blog link above the title that returns readers to the blog index. The site logo links home. Give the author and one date separate, clearly labelled spaces beneath the title. Show Updated with the material revision date when it is later than publication; otherwise show Published with the publication date. Use normal capitalisation for date labels and month names. On desktop, leave 56px between the author/date row and the article body. Keep publication and modification dates in metadata as ISO 8601 timestamps with the Johannesburg time zone. Display only the calendar date; preserve publication-day ordering in the archive. Link the author name to the author profile, where the biography appears.

Blog cards place one labelled date above the topic and title, using normal capitalisation. Show Updated when the article has a later modification date, otherwise Published. Keep the original publication date for archive ordering.

Publication dates follow the owner's approved editorial record. The eleven existing articles use daily dates from 1–11 October 2026 in their original manuscript order, as requested on 27 October; both source date fields use the assigned date and the pages show Published. The schedule is recorded in [editorial-schedule.json](proposals/2026-10-27-article-date-correction/editorial-schedule.json). Date-only editorial assignments use midnight with the Johannesburg offset as a consistent encoding, distinct from a recorded historical clock time.

After that baseline, update an article's modification date when publishing a meaningful revision of its explanations, evidence, examples, recommendations or technical content. Layout, illustration, author-profile, service-link and promotional closing changes leave that article date intact. A local editing pass that never goes live, its reversal and a rebuild also leave the date intact. Keep the visible date, Open Graph dates and structured data aligned with the same source values.

List article sections in an accessible On this page navigation. Keep the sticky desktop panel below the site header when it returns during upward scrolling. Use smaller text than the article body and highlight the section being read. Hover and keyboard focus can highlight another link without replacing that current-section state.

Show the lead image without a visible caption; retain captions for explanatory images in the article body. The compact lead image should convey the article's overall message at thumbnail size. Use short labels when they make the image easier to understand. Keep detailed explanations and larger, text-heavy infographics within the article body.
