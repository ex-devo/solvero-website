Business Improvement Review

Find the changes that will make work easier.

Our Business Improvement Review helps you decide where to act first. We investigate how work happens, identify the causes of repeated effort and agree a practical improvement to take forward.

Discuss an improvement review

Turn a recurring frustration into a decision about what to change.

Your team keeps chasing updates, correcting the same information or waiting for decisions. You can see the effort, but it is harder to tell which change would help most. Adding another tool before understanding the cause can leave the same problem in a new system.

We follow recent examples from request to completion with the people doing the work. We examine handoffs, decisions and system dependencies, including the spreadsheets and workarounds that the written process misses. Together, we distinguish symptoms from causes and identify what needs further evidence.

A practical basis for choosing your next improvement.

A shared picture of the work

A process map showing the agreed boundary, people, systems and decision points. It helps everyone discuss the same workflow and see where work waits or repeats.

Priorities grounded in examples

A list of improvement opportunities with their supporting observations, likely dependencies and unanswered questions. We compare the expected benefit with the effort and disruption involved.

A defined next step

A recommendation describing what to change, who needs to be involved and how to assess the result. Where implementation needs more investigation, that work is made explicit.

How we work with your team.

  1. 01

    Set the boundary

    Choose one business problem and agree what a useful outcome would look like. Identify the process owner, people involved and recent examples we can examine.

  2. 02

    Investigate real cases

    Walk through ordinary and difficult cases. Separate active handling from waiting and rework, then check possible causes with your team and the available evidence.

  3. 03

    Agree the next change

    Compare practical options and their dependencies. Define a manageable next step, its owner and the measures that will help you decide whether it worked.

Start with the problem your team already recognises.

A review might focus on approvals that keep circulating, repeated customer data entry, a difficult onboarding handoff or work that stalls between business and delivery teams. We can also examine how a proposed AI or automation initiative affects the wider process. The review follows the problem across people and systems.

The review produces a decision and a practical plan. Implementation is agreed separately, with responsibilities, access and acceptance criteria defined before the work begins. We can carry the chosen change through process redesign, solution delivery, testing and handover.

For suitable technical changes, explore our business process automation and integration service.

Plan the work with confidence.

What should we bring to the first conversation?

Bring one recurring problem, a recent example and the people who understand the work. Existing process notes or timings are useful; we can establish what further evidence is needed during the review.

Does the answer have to involve new software?

A useful improvement might be a clearer decision, fewer steps, better information at the start or a system change. We choose the response after investigating the cause.

Can you implement the recommendation?

Yes. We can agree a separate implementation scope and deliver the chosen process change, tool or integration. The review does not commit you to a platform or a build.

How do you agree cost and scope?

We establish the process boundary, available evidence, people involved and expected outputs before proposing the work. We agree the scope and commercial terms before starting.

Can you work with our team internationally?

Yes. We are based in Johannesburg and work remotely with English-speaking businesses internationally. We agree meeting times, a shared place for working documents and the people who can make decisions. Discovery uses recent examples and walkthroughs with your team. Access, testing, acceptance and handover are agreed for the scope.
